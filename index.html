Detection of nitrites in water, reaction by reaction

Each section shows one reaction as a step-by-step animation, followed by the chemical equations and what the result means for water quality and health.

Where nitrite comes from

Oxidation of ammonia to nitrate

Nitrite is an intermediate product of the biological oxidation of ammonia into nitrate. Natural water is free from nitrite or seldom contains more than 0.1 mg/L, so any clear presence points to recent fecal pollution.

Equations

OM → NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻
2 NH₄⁺ + 3 O₂ → 2 NO₂⁻ + 4 H⁺ + 2 H₂OStep 1, ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (Nitrosomonas)
2 NO₂⁻ + O₂ → 2 NO₃⁻Step 2, nitrite-oxidizing bacteria (Nitrobacter)

Effect

  • Nitrite present: intermediate stage, recent fecal pollution.
  • Nitrate present with no nitrite: old pollution, oxidation is complete.
  • Sources include sewage disposal systems and livestock facilities.

Qualitative method

Sulphanilic acid and α-naphthylamine

Nitrite is detected by a two-stage diazotization and coupling reaction (Griess reaction) in acid. The end product is a pink azo dye, so a pink color means nitrite is present.

Procedure

  • 50 mL water sample in a colorless cylinder.
  • Add 1 mL sulphanilic acid reagent, shake well, wait 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Add 1 mL α-naphthylamine in HCl and observe.

Reagent: 0.6 g sulphanilic acid + 70 mL hot distilled water + 20 mL conc. HCl, diluted to 100 mL.

Equations

NO₂⁻ + H⁺ ⇌ HNO₂
H₂N–C₆H₄–SO₃H + HNO₂ + H⁺ → [N≡N–C₆H₄–SO₃H]⁺ + 2 H₂ODiazotization of sulphanilic acid
[N≡N–C₆H₄–SO₃H]⁺ + α-naphthylamine → pink azo dye + H⁺Coupling

Effect

  • Pink: positive for NO₂⁻.
  • No pink: negative. This does not prove the water contains exactly zero nitrite.

Quantitative estimation

Zinc iodide starch method

In an acid medium, nitrite oxidizes iodide to iodine. Iodine forms a blue complex with starch. The faster the blue color appears, the more nitrite the sample holds.

Procedure

  • 10 mL sample in a test tube.
  • Add 3 to 5 drops of conc. H₂SO₄.
  • Add 0.5 mL zinc iodide starch solution and start the stopwatch.

Equations

2 NO₂⁻ + 2 I⁻ + 4 H⁺ → I₂ + 2 NO + 2 H₂O
I₂ + I⁻ ⇌ I₃⁻
I₃⁻ + starch → blue starch–iodine complex

Calibration

Blue color appearsNitrite
Immediately0.50 mg/L
After 30 seconds0.30 mg/L
After 1 minute0.15 mg/L

Hygienic significance

Methemoglobin formation

Nitrite oxidizes the iron in hemoglobin from Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺. The resulting methemoglobin cannot carry oxygen, so tissues are starved of it.

Equation

Hb (Fe²⁺) + NO₂⁻ → MetHb (Fe³⁺)Simplified scheme

Effect

  • Fish: brown blood disease. High nitrite with low chloride lowers feed conversion and disease resistance. Calcium, chloride and bicarbonate reduce nitrite toxicity.
  • Humans: blue baby syndrome. Blood loses its ability to carry oxygen, skin and veins look blue.
  • Severe cases lead to digestive and respiratory problems, anoxia, brain damage or death.
  • Adults have enough enzymes to convert methemoglobin back to hemoglobin. Newborns have lower levels and are more susceptible.

Related test for nitrate

Diphenylamine test

Nitrate is colorless and odorless, so it cannot be noticed without a test. Diphenylamine in strong acid is oxidized by nitrate to a blue product.

Procedure

  • In a porcelain dish or Petri dish, place 2 mL diphenylamine reagent.
  • Add a few drops of the water sample.

Equation

NO₃⁻ + diphenylamine (conc. H₂SO₄) → blue oxidation productSimplified scheme

Effect

  • Blue lines or blue rays: positive for nitrate. Light blue corresponds to about 20 to 30 ppm.
  • Risk groups: infants, pregnant women, nursing mothers and elderly people.
  • Ruminants are more susceptible, since rumen bacteria convert nitrate to nitrite.
Safety: concentrated HCl and H₂SO₄ are corrosive and α-naphthylamine needs proper handling. These animations are simulations. Real laboratory work must follow the approved institutional procedure with protective equipment.
